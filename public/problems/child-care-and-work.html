Child care is getting in the way of work

Help parents find care that fits the hours they actually work.

Start with a local conversation

What the evidence shows

Baltimore’s 2025 early childhood landscape analysis describes child care disrupting work and education among families in the Baltimore Young Families Success Fund study. This is evidence from that study, not a measure of every family in the city.

Source: Baltimore City ECAC, 2025 landscape analysis.

Possible solutions to test

These are MD Eagles proposals, not projects already operating. Test the need with residents and existing services before building.

An employer and provider care pilot

Bring a local employer and a licensed child care provider together to test whether a small block of funded care hours fits parents’ shifts. Establish staffing, licensing and costs with the provider before offering places.

A practical care search service

Help families use existing referral services, record where the search breaks down, and share verified availability with permission. Build a new tool only if existing services cannot address the gap.

Your first step

Speak with five parents about the last time care disrupted work. Ask whether cost, opening hours, location or availability was the deciding barrier. Do not assume all four need the same solution.

Who to involve

Parents, licensed providers and an employer in one Baltimore neighborhood.

What to measure

Whether participating parents find suitable care and report fewer shifts missed because of care.

Build on what already exists

Start by learning from Maryland Family Network’s LOCATE service. Ask what they already provide and what remains difficult. No partnership is implied.
