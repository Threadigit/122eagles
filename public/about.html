Why we’re here

What’s harder than it should be in your corner of Maryland?

Working two jobs and still falling behind. Studying between shifts. Keeping a business afloat. Finding care for someone you love. Everyday life deserves a closer look.

Maryland Eagles Eyes is a publication about the challenges facing people across our state—and practical ways residents, businesses and communities can help solve them. The stories live here. Email updates help you keep up.

Our approach

The Maryland Brief

One obstacle. A closer look. A way forward.

Each brief will start with a specific situation in a Maryland community and work through five questions:

  1. Who’s living it? The people affected, in their own words, and what the problem costs them in money, time or opportunity.
  2. What’s getting in the way? The evidence behind the problem, the local context and the questions still unanswered.
  3. What could help? Support available now, approaches tried elsewhere and ideas worth testing—with costs and limitations made clear.
  4. Who can take the next step? Something a resident, employer, founder, community group or public official could do, with a clear starting point.
  5. What changed? Dated follow-ups on progress, setbacks and what remains unresolved.

Find your life in the story.

Our coverage will follow eight parts of everyday life. You may see yourself in more than one.

Making ends meet
You’re working. Why is there still nothing left? Bills, food, unpredictable income and the cost of getting through the month.
Work & what’s next
You need a better job—and a path that fits your life. Career changes, paid training, layoffs and returning to work.
Learning & getting started
You’re building a future while paying for the present. Working students, trade schools, first jobs and second chances at education.
Running a business
You’ve built something. How do you keep it going? Customers, cash flow, late payments, hiring and getting an idea off the ground.
Home & belonging
You need a place you can afford and a community you can call home. Renting, buying, repairs, accessibility and local connections.
Family & caregiving
People depend on you. What support can you depend on? Child care, after-school hours, elder care and life between shifts.
Health & everyday access
Getting help shouldn’t take a day you can’t spare. Care, transport, disability access, internet and services that fit real schedules.
Your neighborhood
You see what’s missing where you live. What would it take to change it? Safe streets, local shops, gathering places and resident-led improvements.

Local enough to be useful.

A good idea has to work where you live. Our aim is to show where each option applies, who can use it, what it costs and where it falls short—from Baltimore and the Washington suburbs to Southern Maryland, Western Maryland and the Eastern Shore.

Students, parents, founders, older adults, immigrants, veterans, disabled Marylanders and people starting over all belong in this coverage. We’ll look at the barriers people face without reducing anyone to their hardest day.

Useful answers. Honest limits.

Our standard is to link to sources, distinguish lived experience from broader evidence, and clearly separate tested approaches from proposals. We’ll make corrections visible and date substantive updates. Some problems need individual action; others need employers, institutions or public policy to change.

A brief should leave you with a better understanding of the problem and a realistic next step—whether you need help, can offer it or want to build something that’s missing.
