# Maryland Eagles Eyes: editorial direction

Publication first; email is how readers keep up. Preserve the welcome page and
bridge identity until there are reported stories ready to publish.

## Signature format: The Maryland Brief

Each brief follows a specific obstacle in a named Maryland community:

1. **Who's living it?** Start with a concrete moment, with consent and accurate
   attribution. Never invent a resident, quote or composite presented as real.
2. **What's getting in the way?** Explain causes with primary sources and local
   evidence. An individual experience is not proof of statewide prevalence.
3. **What could help?** Separate available support, evaluated approaches and
   untested proposals. Verify costs, hours, eligibility, geography and limitations.
4. **Who can take the next step?** Identify realistic actions for residents and
   those able to change conditions: employers, builders, institutions and public
   officials. Don't place responsibility for structural problems on readers.
5. **What changed?** Maintain a dated update log on the original story. Report
   setbacks and no progress as honestly as improvements. Make corrections visible.

Use a consistent story header: location, topic, published/updated dates and a
one-sentence obstacle. Add an action box and source list. Label proposals explicitly.
Don't add fake status trackers, results or endorsements.

## Coverage

Making ends meet; Work & what's next; Learning & getting started; Running a
business; Home & belonging; Family & caregiving; Health & everyday access;
Your neighborhood. These are editorial beats, not eight empty website sections.
Represent different ages, abilities, backgrounds and Maryland regions throughout.

## Launch sequence

Start with three deeply reported briefs, chosen for specificity, evidence,
practical next steps and access to people affected. Candidate angles:

- A shift starts before child care opens: interview parents, providers and employers
  in one locality; verify actual openings, hours and affordability.
- Customers are buying, but invoices aren't paid: follow a local owner's cash-flow
  bottleneck and examine payment practices and support without promising financing.
- Training for better work while still earning: compare real paid/flexible routes,
  entry requirements and outcomes with working students and career changers.

These are leads, not established findings. Before commissioning, ask affected
residents what is missing from the proposed angle. After launch, a verified
contact route can invite: “What's harder than it should be in your corner of
Maryland?” Do not publish a nonfunctional submission form or invented email.

Measure usefulness through reader feedback, verified use of resources and dated
follow-up outcomes, not simply clicks. Avoid sensational headlines and deficit
labels when a precise situation tells the story.

## Publishing and subscription workflow

The existing Buttondown form collects confirmed subscribers. Website deployment
does not automatically send a campaign. No email automation or API credential is
configured by this update, and no subscriber message was sent.

For each new story or meaningful follow-up:
1. Verify sources, consent, resource details, links and publication dates.
2. Publish the page and verify its production URL.
3. Prepare a Buttondown update with the specific obstacle, a useful takeaway and
   a link to the published brief. Check preview and unsubscribe behavior.
4. The publisher sends through Buttondown after reviewing it. An agent needs
   explicit authorization to send to subscribers. Never email for routine code
   pushes, typo fixes or deployments without new editorial material.

Offer free publication updates without a fixed weekly cadence. Future automation
should trigger on an explicit editorial release, deduplicate sends and keep
provider credentials server-side.
